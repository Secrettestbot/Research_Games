About This Study

What We Were Testing

This study examined two competing theories about why people persist in their behavior even when outcomes change:

  1. Festinger's Cognitive Dissonance Theory (1957): People who work harder for a reward value it more highly, leading to greater persistence even after the reward stops.
  2. Capaldi's Sequential Theory (1967): People who experience patterns (like alternating reward and no-reward) develop stronger expectations, leading to greater persistence.

Important Information

Mild Deception: If you participated in the Treasure Hunt game, you should know that in the final phase, ALL chests were actually empty. We wanted to see how long participants would continue trying when rewards stopped. This temporary deception was necessary to test our research questions about persistence.

Why This Research Matters

Understanding what makes people persist (or give up) has important applications in:

  • Education (helping students persist through challenges)
  • Workplace motivation
  • Health behaviors (exercise, diet adherence)
  • Clinical psychology (treating depression and anxiety)
